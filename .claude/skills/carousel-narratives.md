# Carousel Narrative Arcs

Choose one arc before building slides. The arc determines slide order and pacing.

## 𝗟𝗮𝘆𝗲𝗿𝗲𝗱 𝗥𝗲𝘃𝗲𝗮𝗹
- **When to use:** Announcements with multiple "aha" layers, or topics where the real insight is surprising.
- **Structure:** Each slide adds one layer of context, building to a payoff the reader couldn't have predicted from the cover.
- **Why:** The conclusion lands harder when the reader is primed. The surprise is earned, not given away.

## 𝗣𝗿𝗼𝗯𝗹𝗲𝗺 → 𝗦𝗼𝗹𝘂𝘁𝗶𝗼𝗻
- **When to use:** Educational content, tutorials, "here's what most people get wrong."
- **Structure:** Open with the pain point. Each middle slide = one step toward the fix. Last slide = the full picture.
- **Why:** Hooks on relatable friction, pays off with value. Strong Save driver.

## 𝗠𝘆𝘁𝗵 𝘃𝘀. 𝗥𝗲𝗮𝗹𝗶𝘁𝘆
- **When to use:** Contrarian takes, industry misconceptions, "everyone thinks X but actually Y."
- **Structure:** Alternating slides: myth → reality → myth → reality. Rhythm creates momentum.
- **Why:** Generates debate and shares. Each myth is a hook; each reality is the payoff.

## 𝗟𝗶𝘀𝘁𝗶𝗰𝗹𝗲
- **When to use:** Tips, tools, quick wins, "N things you didn't know about X."
- **Structure:** Cover announces the number. Each middle slide = one self-contained item. Last slide = summary or CTA.
- **Why:** Scannable, shareable, easy to save. Works for any topic.

## 𝗕𝗲𝗳𝗼𝗿𝗲 / 𝗔𝗳𝘁𝗲𝗿
- **When to use:** Transformation stories, product showcases, process improvements.
- **Structure:** First half = the "before" world. Second half = the "after." Last slide = the delta.
- **Why:** Contrast is visceral. Readers feel the gap before they understand it intellectually.

## 𝗦𝘁𝗲𝗽-𝗯𝘆-𝗦𝘁𝗲𝗽
- **When to use:** Tutorials, how-tos, sequential processes.
- **Structure:** Each slide = one step, numbered. Last slide = result or outcome.
- **Why:** Clear, actionable, high Save rate. Readers return when they implement.
